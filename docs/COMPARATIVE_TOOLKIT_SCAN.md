# Comparative PFAC Toolkit Scan

This scan supports the APE PFAC PSSM 5 Leadership Toolkit. It identifies existing public PFAC and patient/family engagement toolkits from government, nonprofit, hospital, and academic medical center contexts. The purpose is not to copy these tools. The purpose is to learn from them and make this APE product more useful for Dartmouth Health and other health systems.

## Design conclusion

Existing PFAC toolkits are strong on startup mechanics, advisor recruitment, meeting structure, and general patient/family engagement. The opportunity for this APE toolkit is to make the work more operational and patient-first by adding four things:

1. A clear PSSM 5 Patient and Family Engagement structure.
2. A Press Ganey-style patient experience metric logic layer.
3. A loved-one test anchored in Rosie Bartel's lived-experience story.
4. A leadership action loop that requires ownership, due dates, documented follow-through, and reassessment.

## Highest-value public toolkits and resources

### 1. AHRQ Guide to Patient and Family Engagement in Hospital Quality and Safety

What it offers: A tested, evidence-based resource for hospitals to partner with patients and families to improve quality and safety. It includes implementation and evaluation guidance, leadership support material, and customizable tools.

What this APE toolkit learns from it: The APE toolkit should stay practical and implementation-oriented, not just conceptual. It should include tools leaders can tailor and use.

How this APE toolkit improves on it: Add a PSSM 5 alignment layer, patient-first harm-benefit screen, PFAC maturity dashboard, and direct mapping to patient experience metrics.

### 2. AHRQ Strategy 1: Working With Patients and Families as Advisors

What it offers: Practical guidance for forming advisory councils, including eligibility criteria, roles, mission statements, bylaws, and council involvement.

What this APE toolkit learns from it: A PFAC must be a formal collaboration structure, not a support group, grievance committee, staff meeting, or presentation forum.

How this APE toolkit improves on it: Add stronger leadership accountability and require patient benefit, patient harm risk, and follow-through documentation for each PFAC recommendation.

### 3. IPFCC Reflecting Our Community: A Toolkit for Strengthening PFACs

What it offers: Guidance to strengthen PFAC representativeness, create a welcoming environment, recruit representative PFAs, engage PFAs in health equity, and sustain progress.

What this APE toolkit learns from it: PFAC effectiveness depends on representativeness, belonging, equity, and sustained engagement.

How this APE toolkit improves on it: Connect representativeness and equity to PSSM 5 structure, patient experience metric stratification, and visible leadership action.

### 4. AHRQ CUSP Patient and Family Engagement Module

What it offers: Patient and family engagement tools focused on patients understanding what is happening during hospitalization, participating in care, preparing for discharge, working with patient advisors, communication, and adverse event communication.

What this APE toolkit learns from it: Patient experience must include safety, communication, discharge, and adverse event transparency.

How this APE toolkit improves on it: Add the loved-one test and make PFAC feedback a standing leadership control for high-risk patient journey moments.

### 5. AHRQ Family-Centered Rounds Toolkit, University of Wisconsin-Madison and American Family Children's Hospital

What it offers: A toolkit developed at a pediatric academic medical center with family representatives, pediatric professionals, and human factors/systems engineering researchers. It includes a checklist, training curriculum, materials, data collection tools, and implementation recommendations.

What this APE toolkit learns from it: Practical toolkits should include workflow artifacts, training materials, and measurement tools, not only narrative guidance.

How this APE toolkit improves on it: Generalize the idea beyond pediatric rounds into an executive PFAC/PSSM 5 operating system for patient experience improvement.

### 6. AMA STEPS Forward PFAC Toolkit

What it offers: Practical steps for forming a PFAC, including leadership support, member interview/selection, and starting the council's first project.

What this APE toolkit learns from it: A useful toolkit must help leaders move from idea to launch.

How this APE toolkit improves on it: Add PSSM 5 traceability, patient safety story anchor, and patient experience measurement logic.

### 7. Betsy Lehman Center PFAC Resource Library

What it offers: A large curated library of PFAC guidelines, tools, research, examples, and leader resources, including resources on rebuilding PFACs, diversity, quality and safety, recruitment, and metrics.

What this APE toolkit learns from it: Dartmouth Health and other researchers need a resource layer, not only a single static document.

How this APE toolkit improves on it: Turn the resource library idea into a focused PSSM 5 leadership product with patient-first decision screens.

### 8. Brigham and Women's Hospital patient-centered toolkit work

What it offers: An academic medical center example of participatory design with patients and family advisory council input to build patient-centered tools for hospitalized patients and care partners.

What this APE toolkit learns from it: Patients and care partners should shape interface requirements, workflow design, and usability.

How this APE toolkit improves on it: Apply participatory design thinking to PFAC governance and leadership decisions, not just a single patient-facing tool.

### 9. Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center, IPFCC, and patient advisory councils in learning health systems

What it offers: Examples of patient advisory councils contributing to toolkits, research partnerships, health equity work, and learning health systems.

What this APE toolkit learns from it: PFACs can help generate patient-led resources and support learning health system cycles.

How this APE toolkit improves on it: Make the learning loop explicit: capture, interpret, act, document, report back, reassess.

## Implementation requirements for the APE product

The website should include:

1. Patient-first landing page.
2. Rosie Bartel acknowledgement and story anchor.
3. Evidence and bibliography page.
4. Open resources page.
5. PFAC/PSSM 5 toolkit page.
6. Patient experience metric driver page.
7. Competency proof page tied to the APE Agreement.
8. API routes for toolkit content and open resources.

The toolkit should include:

1. PFAC current-state assessment.
2. PFAC maturity model.
3. Recruitment and representation checklist.
4. Co-design meeting guide.
5. Patient-first decision screen.
6. You said, we did tracker.
7. Quarterly patient experience metric review.
8. PSSM 5 traceability checklist.
