# Rosie Bartel Story Anchor

This is a story and resource anchor, not a research evidence claim layer.

## Why this belongs in the toolkit

This APE project is grounded in patient point of view. Rosie Bartel's public story is the clearest lived-experience anchor for why PFACs, PSSM 5, patient safety, infection prevention, and patient-family voice cannot become check-the-box work.

The personal frame is also explicit: a loved one preparing for total knee replacement makes this work more than an academic deliverable. The practical test is whether the health system would be safe, clear, humane, and accountable enough for my mother or someone I love.

## Publicly documented story facts

Rosie Bartel is a Wisconsin patient advocate, survivor, educator, widow, mother, and grandmother. Public profiles from Sepsis Alliance describe that in August 2009 she underwent a total right knee replacement that developed into a MRSA staph infection. Her public story reports 58 surgeries, more than 200 hospitalizations, more than 100 blood transfusions, right leg amputation above the knee, later total hip amputation with removal of part of the pelvic bone, and repeated sepsis and septic shock episodes.

Her advocacy message is not abstract. It is a direct warning that a routine procedure can become life altering when infection prevention, listening, escalation, care coordination, and patient-family voice fail.

## How this changes the toolkit language

The toolkit should not say only: improve patient experience metrics.

The toolkit should say: improve patient experience metrics because behind every score is a real person who may be frightened, infected, unheard, harmed, discharged too early, or left to navigate life-changing complications.

The toolkit should not say only: comply with PSSM 5.

The toolkit should say: use PSSM 5 as a structural safety commitment so patient and family voice has authority before harm, during care, after discharge, and during system redesign.

## Patient-first operating questions

1. Would this be safe enough for my mother before, during, and after total knee replacement?
2. Would the patient or family know what infection warning signs require urgent escalation?
3. Would staff listen if the patient or family says something is wrong?
4. Would the system detect early deterioration before the patient has to prove they are in danger?
5. Would the PFAC have enough authority to change the workflow, not just comment on it?
6. Would we document what patients said, what leaders did, and what changed?
7. Would this be acceptable if the patient were me or someone I love?

## Implementation rule

Rosie Bartel's story should be used as a lived-experience anchor and public patient-safety resource. It should not be treated as a systematic research source. Formal evidence and AMA bibliography remain separate, but the story explains why the toolkit exists and why patient voice must be operationalized.
