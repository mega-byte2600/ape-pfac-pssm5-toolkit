# PFAC Toolkit Model Scan

Purpose: identify existing public PFAC, patient engagement, academic medical center, hospital, and safety toolkits that the APE PFAC PSSM 5 Leadership Toolkit can learn from, reference, and improve on.

## Bottom line

Several strong PFAC and patient engagement resources already exist. The gap this project fills is not “another PFAC explanation.” The gap is an industry-grade, patient-first, PSSM 5-specific leadership toolkit that connects PFAC structure to patient experience improvement, Press Ganey-style metric families, infection-prevention escalation, closed-loop action, and public reusable resources.

## What exists and what we should learn

### 1. AHRQ Guide to Patient and Family Engagement in Hospital Quality and Safety

AHRQ provides a tested, evidence-based guide for hospitals to partner with patients and families to improve quality and safety. It includes strategy areas for patients and families as advisors, communication, nurse bedside shift report, and IDEAL discharge planning.

Learn from it: make the toolkit practical and implementation-oriented, not just conceptual.

Improve on it: add direct PSSM 5 alignment, Press Ganey-style metric mapping, a patient-harm screen, and a public API/source layer.

### 2. AHRQ Strategy 1: Working With Patients and Families as Advisors

AHRQ provides advisor-facing materials such as implementation handbooks, orientation materials, confidentiality statements, and advisor role content.

Learn from it: advisors need onboarding, role clarity, and safe operating rules.

Improve on it: connect advisor work to executive sponsorship, measurable actions, and visible feedback to patients.

### 3. IPFCC Reflecting Our Community Toolkit

IPFCC focuses on strengthening PFACs so they reflect and support the communities hospitals serve, including representativeness, welcoming environments, health equity partnership, action planning, and sustainability.

Learn from it: PFAC legitimacy depends on who is included, who feels welcome, and whether equity is operationalized.

Improve on it: make representativeness and equity part of a scored PFAC maturity model tied to experience metric variation.

### 4. AMA STEPS Forward PFAC Toolkit with Johns Hopkins influence

AMA STEPS Forward provides practical steps for forming PFACs, including leadership support, interviewing and selecting members, and starting the council’s first project. The related AMA and Johns Hopkins recruitment/onboarding toolkit supports advisor recruitment and orientation.

Learn from it: leadership support, recruitment, onboarding, and a first project are essential.

Improve on it: add the Rosie Bartel loved-one test, infection/escalation scenario, PSSM 5 scope control, and action accountability.

### 5. Johns Hopkins Medicine PFACs

Johns Hopkins Medicine describes PFACs across hospitals, care settings, community physicians, emergency medicine, and specialized centers. The model shows that PFACs can operate beyond one generic council.

Learn from it: PFACs can be scaled across service lines and sites.

Improve on it: translate PFAC structure into a reusable source-to-action toolkit for PSSM 5 and patient experience metrics.

### 6. Massachusetts General Hospital PFACs

Mass General frames PFACs as a primary way to realize its mission language around being guided by the needs of patients and families and connects patient voice to care experience, including HCAHPS feedback.

Learn from it: mission language and experience data should connect to PFAC operations.

Improve on it: make the connection explicit between story, PFAC input, metric movement, and quarterly leadership action review.

### 7. AHRQ MRSA Prevention Toolkit: Targeting SSI

AHRQ’s MRSA toolkit is directly relevant to cardiac, hip and knee joint replacement, and spinal fusion surgeries. It focuses on MRSA and surgical site infection prevention.

Learn from it: infection prevention and escalation are not abstract safety topics for this project; they connect directly to Rosie Bartel’s knee replacement story and the user’s loved-one test.

Improve on it: use PFACs to review whether patients and families understand infection warning signs, escalation pathways, hand hygiene expectations, and post-discharge danger signals.

### 8. Leapfrog PFAC Toolkit for Exploring Diagnostic Quality

Leapfrog shows how PFACs can be used to examine a specific patient safety domain through structured exercises and guidance.

Learn from it: PFAC toolkits work best when they are issue-specific.

Improve on it: make this project issue-specific to PSSM 5, patient experience improvement, and infection/escalation as a lived-experience scenario.

### 9. Betsy Lehman Center PFAC Resource Library

The Betsy Lehman Center curates PFAC resources, hospital examples, and PFAC strengthening materials, especially for patient safety and Massachusetts hospital PFAC work.

Learn from it: open resource libraries help researchers and health systems benchmark and reuse materials.

Improve on it: create an open resources page and API so Dartmouth Health and other researchers can reuse, validate, and extend the toolkit.

## Design requirements for this project

1. Rosie Bartel is the first acknowledgement and lived-experience anchor.
2. Research evidence and AMA 11 bibliography remain separate from story/resources.
3. PSSM 5 scope is locked.
4. Press Ganey-style metric families are treated as improvement signals, not reputation management.
5. Every tool must answer patient benefit, patient harm risk, and the loved-one test.
6. PFAC output must produce action, owner, due date, decision rationale, and follow-up.
7. The open resources layer must remain public and reusable.
