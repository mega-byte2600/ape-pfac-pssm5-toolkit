# Rosie Bartel Excerpt Selection

Rosie Bartel is the most important acknowledgement and lived-experience source for this APE project. This file documents the selected excerpt logic so the project keeps the difference clear between:

1. formal evidence and bibliography, and
2. patient story and lived-experience resources.

## Selected project anchor

> What matters to a patient is to survive and to survive it with some dignity and respect.

## Why this excerpt was selected

This is the strongest project anchor because it compresses the entire purpose of the PFAC/PSSM 5 toolkit into one patient-first standard. It is not only about preventing infection. It is about survival, dignity, respect, listening, escalation, and system accountability.

For this APE, the quote supports the core design rule:

If a health system would not accept the process, communication, follow-through, or risk for my mother or someone I love, the process is not good enough.

## How it changes the toolkit

The toolkit cannot be limited to reputation management or score chasing. Patient experience metrics must be treated as signals of safety, trust, dignity, coordination, equity, and harm prevention.

The PFAC must therefore have enough structure to do more than comment. It must help leaders:

1. identify where patients and families are at risk,
2. listen when patients or families say something is wrong,
3. detect infection and deterioration concerns early,
4. improve communication and discharge readiness,
5. translate patient stories into action,
6. report back what changed, and
7. reassess whether the change improved patient experience and safety.

## Audio resource note

Do not copy, rehost, or redistribute podcast audio without permission. The website should link to the original podcast page and, where possible, identify a short listening window for reviewers.

The selected listening window should highlight Rosie's voice explaining that patients want to survive with dignity and respect, and that everyday operational work can prevent people from becoming sick or having their life changed forever.
