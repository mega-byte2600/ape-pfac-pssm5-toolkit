# ILE Research Blueprint

## Purpose

This document defines the scholarly bridge from the completed Applied Practice Experience work into a future Integrative Learning Experience. It is a research planning artifact, not a submitted ILE paper and not a substitute for faculty-directed ILE assignments.

The APE remains an applied, non-research practice project. The ILE is the separate scholarly layer that can use the APE as its substantive foundation while applying a more formal research question, literature synthesis, analytic framework, and academic writing standard.

## Proposed ILE focus

**Working title:** From Patient Voice to Accountable Health-System Action: Operationalizing Patient and Family Engagement Under the CMS Patient Safety Structural Measure

### Core problem

Health systems increasingly formalize patient and family engagement through Patient and Family Advisory Councils and related structures. The existence of a PFAC, however, does not by itself establish that patient input changes governance, safety practice, experience, equity, or outcomes. CMS Patient Safety Structural Measure Domain 5 raises the operational importance of patient and family engagement, but organizations still need a defensible way to translate formal engagement structures into accountable action without overstating causal evidence.

### Primary research question

**What organizational, governance, and implementation conditions are associated with effective translation of formal patient and family engagement structures into accountable health-system action, and how can those conditions be operationalized within CMS Patient Safety Structural Measure Domain 5?**

### Secondary questions

1. What functions distinguish symbolic or consultative patient engagement from engagement that is integrated into health-system decision making?
2. What governance features recur across credible PFAC and patient-and-family-engagement frameworks?
3. What measures have been used to evaluate engagement process, representativeness, implementation, and downstream organizational response?
4. Where is the evidence strong enough to support action, and where are claims limited by study design, context, or lack of causal evidence?
5. How can health-system leaders create a traceable pathway from patient and family input to ownership, action, measurement, and report-back?

## Recommended scholarly design

### Design

A structured evidence synthesis with an implementation-oriented analytic framework.

The preferred design is a **scoping review or structured integrative review**, depending on faculty guidance and final eligibility criteria. This design is more defensible than attempting to infer local outcome effects from the APE because the current APE was not designed as a causal research study.

### Why this design fits

The project question spans heterogeneous evidence, including empirical studies, systematic reviews, implementation studies, federal policy, consensus frameworks, and operational toolkits. A structured evidence synthesis can characterize the field, identify recurring implementation conditions, distinguish evidence levels, map gaps, and derive a bounded leadership framework without claiming that PFACs independently cause improved outcomes.

## Conceptual model

The ILE should test and refine the following pathway:

**Representation and access → governance authority → structured input → accountable ownership → implementation → measurement → report-back and learning**

This is an analytic model, not a causal claim. Each link should be assessed for the strength and type of supporting evidence.

## Evidence domains

The synthesis should organize evidence into six domains:

1. **Representation and inclusion**
   - recruitment
   - demographic and experiential diversity
   - barriers to participation
   - compensation and access

2. **Governance and authority**
   - reporting relationships
   - leadership sponsorship
   - decision rights
   - escalation pathways

3. **Engagement process**
   - meeting structure
   - agenda setting
   - co-design
   - bidirectional communication

4. **Action and accountability**
   - assignment of ownership
   - implementation tracking
   - documented disposition of recommendations
   - report-back to patients and families

5. **Measurement and learning**
   - process measures
   - representation measures
   - implementation measures
   - patient experience and safety measures
   - limits of attribution

6. **Policy and structural alignment**
   - CMS Patient Safety Structural Measure Domain 5
   - federal patient and family engagement guidance
   - learning health system principles

## Source hierarchy

Use a deliberate source hierarchy rather than treating all materials as equivalent:

1. systematic reviews and meta-analyses
2. peer-reviewed empirical studies
3. implementation and evaluation studies
4. federal requirements and guidance
5. consensus frameworks and professional guidance
6. health-system implementation examples
7. lived-experience material used only as contextual or design insight

Lived experience should never be presented as representative empirical evidence.

## Eligibility framework

### Include

- hospital, health-system, and clinically integrated care settings
- formal patient and family engagement structures
- PFACs, patient advisory councils, co-design structures, and closely related governance mechanisms
- studies or frameworks addressing implementation, governance, representation, measurement, patient experience, safety, equity, or organizational learning
- English-language evidence unless faculty approves a broader language strategy
- peer-reviewed literature plus selected authoritative grey literature needed for policy and implementation context

### Exclude

- engagement limited to individual treatment decisions with no organizational or system-level component
- purely conceptual commentary with no useful implementation content unless foundational
- marketing materials
- claims that cannot be traced to an identifiable source
- local APE observations treated as research findings

## Data extraction framework

For each included source, extract:

- citation
- year
- jurisdiction / setting
- study or source type
- population / organizational context
- engagement structure
- governance mechanism
- implementation mechanism
- measurement approach
- reported outcomes or effects
- key limitations
- evidence strength
- relevance to CMS PSSM Domain 5
- relevance to the proposed action-accountability pathway

## Analytic strategy

### Phase 1: Evidence mapping

Describe the distribution of evidence by study type, setting, intervention or engagement structure, and outcome category.

### Phase 2: Thematic synthesis

Code recurrent implementation conditions across the six evidence domains. Preserve negative, null, or conflicting findings rather than forcing convergence.

### Phase 3: Claim calibration

Classify major conclusions as:

- well supported
- supported with important limitations
- plausible but under-evidenced
- not supported by current evidence

### Phase 4: Framework derivation

Use the synthesis to refine the proposed pathway from representation to measurable organizational action. Each component of the final framework should be traceable to the evidence base.

## Role of existing APE assets

The current APE contributes infrastructure and applied context but should not be treated as primary research data.

Useful inputs include:

- environmental scan
- evidence matrix
- annotated bibliography and surveillance workflow
- comparative toolkit scan
- CMS Domain 5 traceability work
- Dartmouth Health benchmark framework
- Upper Valley descriptive population analysis
- leadership tools and governance templates
- lived-experience design anchor

The ILE should critically re-evaluate these assets through a scholarly evidence standard rather than simply describing the APE.

## Local Dartmouth Health boundary

Publicly available Dartmouth Health information may be used as organizational context or an implementation example when appropriately cited. The ILE should not assign a Dartmouth Health maturity score, infer internal compliance status, or make claims about internal performance without validated data and appropriate authorization.

The existing Upper Valley analysis is descriptive context. It should not be used to infer PFAC effectiveness or causal relationships.

## Research and IRB boundary

The literature-based design does not require collection of private patient information or new human-subject data. If the ILE later adds interviews, surveys, identifiable operational data, internal patient experience data, or systematic collection of information from individuals, the project scope should be reviewed with the faculty mentor and the appropriate institutional research or IRB process before data collection begins.

## Proposed manuscript architecture

1. Introduction
2. Background and policy context
3. Research question and objectives
4. Methods
5. Results: evidence map
6. Results: implementation and governance themes
7. Results: measurement and attribution limitations
8. Proposed evidence-to-action framework
9. Discussion
10. Implications for health-system leaders
11. Limitations
12. Conclusion

## Candidate contribution

The potential scholarly contribution is not a claim that PFACs improve outcomes. The stronger contribution is a **traceable evidence-to-action model** that clarifies what health systems must put around formal patient and family engagement structures for those structures to influence governance and improvement work in an accountable, measurable way.

That contribution would extend the APE from a practical toolkit into a scholarly synthesis of the conditions under which patient and family engagement can function as part of a learning health system.

## Quality gates

Before any ILE manuscript section is considered mature, confirm:

- every factual assertion is traceable to a source
- evidence type is explicit
- causal language matches study design
- PFAC structure is not equated with improved outcomes
- CMS requirements are separated from empirical evidence
- Dartmouth Health context is not represented as validated internal research
- lived experience is not conflated with population-level evidence
- limitations and contradictory evidence are preserved
- AMA citations are complete and consistent
- the final research question remains narrower than the APE toolkit as a whole
