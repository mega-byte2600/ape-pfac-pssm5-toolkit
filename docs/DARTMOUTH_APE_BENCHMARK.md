# Dartmouth / Geisel APE Benchmark Standard

This benchmark applies only to the Applied Practice Experience and judges whether the APE is complete, useful to the host site, methodologically defensible, and demonstrably tied to the selected competencies.

## What Dartmouth publicly emphasizes for APE work

Current Dartmouth Health Sciences and Geisel descriptions consistently frame the APE as a hands-on, field-based experience in which the student applies MPH knowledge and competencies to a real health or healthcare problem in collaboration with a host site. Strong projects generate actionable insights, practical tools, recommendations, analyses, or implementation products that are useful to the site.

Public Dartmouth APE examples show several recurring patterns:

1. **Defined host-site problem.** The work starts with a concrete operational, population-health, quality, safety, workforce, access, or policy problem.
2. **Applied method.** Students use a method appropriate to the problem, including environmental scans, literature reviews, data analysis, interviews, surveys, workflow mapping, program analysis, mixed methods, or quality-improvement methods.
3. **Tangible site product.** Examples include guides, toolkits, frameworks, measurement approaches, policy recommendations, resource-allocation analyses, intervention designs, and implementation resources.
4. **Visible decision value.** The strongest examples show how the host site can use the work to allocate resources, change workflow, improve access, strengthen policy, select measures, or implement an intervention.
5. **Competency development through applied work.** The product is evidence that the student can do public-health work, not merely describe a topic.

## Relevant Dartmouth comparators

### Laura Galster, MPH '23
Health Equity Measurement in Hospitals and Health Systems: An Environmental Scan.

Why it matters here: Dartmouth explicitly presents an environmental scan as exemplary practicum work. The differentiator is not whether the method is called research. It is whether the scan produces a structured, useful synthesis for a health-system problem.

### Jonathan Weatherly, MPH '24
Making Data Work: Review of Mobile Asthma Clinic Use of a Health Atlas to Support Resource Allocation Decisions and Outcomes Measurement.

Why it matters here: the project connected an organizational need to data gaps, geographic and demographic analysis, and concrete recommendations for resource allocation and outcomes measurement.

### Nicole Winn, MPH '25
Developing Tools and Resources for Long-Term Care Facilities.

Why it matters here: the student combined statewide data analysis and staff interviews with practical guides that were distributed to long-term-care facilities. The product moved beyond evidence review into direct field utility.

### Pranati Kuchimanchi, MPH '26
The AI Prescription: The Role of AI in Advancing Workforce Retention and Medication Safety in Remote Hospitals.

Why it matters here: workflow mapping, interviews, leadership collaboration, and a structured 38-indicator framework transformed a broad topic into a host-site tool with a defined future pilot path.

### Dartmouth 2021-2022 examples
Projects included COVID-19 analytics, detoxification-facility secondary data analysis, statewide cost and quality benchmarks, employee-needs assessment, integrated-care measurement, and qualitative analysis of staff survey data.

Why they matter here: strong APEs do not share one method. They share a defensible link between a field problem, applied evidence or data, interpretation, and something the host site can use.

## Faculty benchmark for this APE

The PFAC/PSSM project is potentially stronger than many public examples in breadth, reproducibility, traceability, and digital usability. Breadth alone does not make it better. To qualify as best-in-class, the final APE must make the following chain unmistakable:

**Dartmouth Health problem → evidence and local asset assessment → interpretation → two approved deliverables → leadership decisions/actions → competency attainment.**

### Deliverable 1: Environmental scan + annotated bibliography

Faculty-ready when it includes:

- a precise applied question;
- reproducible search and grey-literature methods;
- source screening and classification;
- critical appraisal or evidence-strength judgment;
- explicit contradictory/uncertain evidence;
- comparative findings across engagement approaches;
- an executive synthesis that states what Dartmouth Health can reasonably learn and what cannot yet be concluded.

### Deliverable 2: PFAC leadership toolkit/playbook

Faculty-ready when it includes actual reusable tools, not only explanatory webpages:

- current-state assessment;
- PSSM Domain 5 traceability;
- PFAC governance/authority assessment;
- representation and access checklist;
- co-design or meeting guide;
- patient-first decision screen;
- closed-loop action tracker;
- measurement/reassessment guide;
- implementation instructions and ownership.

## What would place this project above the public examples

1. **Evidence calibration.** Explicitly state where PFAC outcome evidence is weak or uncertain instead of advocating beyond the literature.
2. **Local applicability without overclaiming.** Benchmark Dartmouth Health assets using evidence-state labels and publish only what can be supported.
3. **True toolkit usability.** Provide downloadable or directly usable assessment/checklist/tracker artifacts rather than relying on narrative pages.
4. **Visible competency evidence.** For each selected competency, point to a specific completed analysis, comparison, decision tool, or applied artifact.
5. **Scope discipline.** Keep every public and internal APE artifact focused on the approved site work, two practical deliverables, and competency attainment.
6. **Host-site handoff.** End with a concise 'what Dartmouth Health can do next' page showing owner, action, evidence needed, and implementation sequence.

## Current faculty judgment

The project already exceeds typical public Dartmouth examples in technical architecture, transparency of evidence status, reproducibility infrastructure, and breadth of reusable resources. It does **not yet clearly exceed the strongest examples in applied public-health proof** because the current site still has incomplete local assessment evidence, incomplete population needs/assets evidence, and insufficiently explicit data-analysis proof for CEPH 4.

The goal is therefore not to add more pages. The goal is to finish the evidence-to-decision chain and make the two approved deliverables unmistakably usable by the host site.
