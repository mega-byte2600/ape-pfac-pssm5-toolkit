# Project Acknowledgement

This MPH Applied Practice Experience project is dedicated first and foremost to Rosie Bartel.

Rosie Bartel's lived experience after total knee replacement, healthcare-associated MRSA infection, sepsis, amputation, and long-term patient safety advocacy is the patient story anchor for this toolkit. Without her public witness, advocacy, and willingness to turn harm into system learning, this project would not have the same purpose, urgency, or moral center.

Her story is recognized in this project as a core source of meaning and design discipline. The formal research bibliography explains what the evidence supports. Rosie Bartel's story explains why the toolkit must stay patient first.

## Locked project rule

Every PFAC, PSSM 5, patient experience, Press Ganey, and leadership design decision must pass the loved-one test:

1. How does this benefit the patient or family?
2. How could this hurt the patient or family if the system gets it wrong?
3. Would this be acceptable if the patient were me, my mother, or someone I love?

## Patient-first motto

Patients over payers, bureaucracy, internal convenience, and checkbox compliance.

## How this acknowledgement should be used

Rosie Bartel's story belongs in the acknowledgement, story, and resources layer of the toolkit. It should not be reduced to an academic citation or treated as background color. It is a patient-safety anchor that keeps the entire project honest.

The research bibliography remains the evidence layer. Rosie Bartel's story remains the patient voice and moral center of the project.
