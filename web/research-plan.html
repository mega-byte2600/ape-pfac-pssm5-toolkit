Environmental scan • PubMed • grey literature • reproducible screening

Evidence-review protocol for a non-research APE.

This method supports the approved environmental scan. It documents how peer-reviewed evidence, federal guidance, public health-system resources, and implementation toolkits are identified, screened, extracted, appraised, and translated into leadership decisions.

QuestionHow are PFACs structured, governed, measured, and used to support patient and family engagement, safety, experience, equity, and co-design?
Primary evidence sourcePubMed, supplemented by government and health-system grey literature.
OutputEnvironmental scan, annotated bibliography, evidence matrix, comparative toolkit scan, and leadership tools.

Eligibility

What enters the evidence layer.

Population

Patients, families, advisors, and care teams

PFACs, patient advisors, family advisors, co-design, co-production, patient engagement, patient experience, patient safety, and related governance structures.

Setting

Hospitals, ambulatory care, and health systems

Priority is given to health-system, inpatient, ambulatory, safety, quality-improvement, and learning-health-system contexts.

Evidence value

Relevant even when findings are weak

Sources may inform governance, implementation, measurement, equity, patient benefit, risk, or limitations. Conflicting, null, or negative findings are retained when they materially affect leadership interpretation.

Bias control

Designed to reduce confirmation bias.

Do not select for success

Actionability is not an inclusion requirement.

Evidence is not excluded merely because it fails to show benefit or offers no immediate implementation recommendation.

Separate evidence types

Do not treat guidance as outcomes evidence.

Federal requirements, implementation guidance, empirical studies, conceptual frameworks, local hypotheses, and lived experience are labeled separately.

Record uncertainty

Limitations travel with the claim.

Each evidence-matrix row records evidence type, strength, limitation, and whether the stated patient benefit is observed or anticipated.

Grey literature

Sources used for implementation and benchmark context.

Federal

CMS and AHRQ

Structural measure requirements, patient and family engagement guidance, implementation resources, and publicly available measures.

Professional

IPFCC and comparable toolkits

Established PFAC implementation practices are reviewed as guidance and benchmark context, not as causal outcomes evidence.

Health systems

Publicly documented operating models

Public health-system PFAC charters, structures, recruitment practices, and engagement models are extracted when sufficiently documented.

Screening workflow

How each source is handled.

Step 1

Search log and deduplication

Record database or source, exact query, search date, result count, and duplicate removal before screening.

Step 2

Title and abstract screen

Retain sources relevant to PFAC design, governance, representation, patient/family engagement, safety, experience, co-design, implementation, or measurement.

Step 3

Full-text review and extraction

Extract design, setting, population, engagement model, findings, metrics, limitations, implementation implications, and source citation.

Step 4

Evidence-strength appraisal

Classify source type and record major limitations, including observational design, self-report, attribution limits, narrow settings, or lack of outcome measurement.

Step 5

Evidence-to-action synthesis

Translate only what the source supports. Distinguish empirical findings from leadership recommendations and anticipated patient benefit.

Step 6

Version control

Maintain accepted sources and annotations in Zotero and refresh the public bibliography, matrix, and toolkit only after review.
