AHRQ PSSM Domain 5 Federal patient safety structural measure guidance Patient and Family Engagement structure, including PFAC input on safety. Patient and family voice becomes part of safety infrastructure. Engagement becomes a paper exercise without influence on safety practice. Review safety priorities, escalation gaps, and patient-facing risk communication. Assign executive ownership for PFAC safety input and follow-through. PSSM 5 attestation, safety culture, experience trust signals.
AHRQ Guide to Patient and Family Engagement Federal implementation toolkit Practical hospital tools for partnering with patients and families. Improves communication, discharge readiness, bedside engagement, and care partnership. Families remain passive during risk, transition, and communication failures. Co-design discharge, bedside communication, and plain-language materials. Adopt a structured implementation plan and review results with PFAC members. Communication, discharge, teamwork, safety perception, overall rating.
Oldfield et al, 2019 Systematic review PFACs as structured engagement mechanisms in health care and research. Patient, family, and community perspectives receive a formal pathway into improvement. PFACs can become tokenistic, underpowered, or disconnected from outcomes. Define representation, recruitment, agenda-setting, and documentation practices. Create governance rules, meeting cadence, and decision escalation pathways. PFAC maturity, equity reach, patient experience themes, implementation evidence.
Sharma et al, 2017 Systematic review Patient advisors can influence health care outcomes when linked to action and measurement. Patient input is more likely to produce visible change. Patients share lived experience without seeing results or accountability. Track what patients raised, what changed, and what remains unresolved. Use a closed-loop tracker and report back to PFAC and leaders quarterly. Action closure rate, experience themes, complaint patterns, trust.
Batalden et al, 2016 Conceptual quality and service framework Healthcare service is co-produced through relationships and activities between professionals and people served. Care design becomes clearer, safer, more humane, and more responsive. Care can be technically delivered while still feeling confusing, fragmented, or disrespectful. Use co-design sessions before workflows are finalized. Build patient voice into design decisions, not after-the-fact review. Dignity, communication, trust, teamwork, overall rating.
Batalden and Foster, 2021 Quality 3.0 framing Movement from assurance/compliance toward service value and co-produced health. Improvement work focuses on value experienced by the person receiving care. Organizations over-focus on compliance while missing lived experience and service value. Ask whether PFAC work changes the actual care experience. Frame PFACs as operating infrastructure for value, not courtesy committees. Value, experience, safety, engagement, follow-through.
Joseph et al, 2023 Scoping review Patient journey mapping can support learning health system feedback loops. Care gaps become visible across access, care, discharge, and follow-up. Leaders optimize isolated touchpoints while missing the full patient journey. Map patient journeys and identify pain points by population and setting. Prioritize redesign where journey failures produce safety or experience risk. LTR, overall rating, access, transitions, communication.
Oliver et al, 2019 Patient-reported data action framework Feedback and feed-forward processes can convert patient-reported data into decisions. Patient reports are interpreted and used before problems become normalized. Signals accumulate without action, causing repeated confusion, delay, distrust, or harm. Review patient-reported themes and recommend targeted tests of change. Run capture, interpret, act, document, and reassess cycles. Patient-reported experience, safety perception, complaints, action completion.
CDC and AHRQ HAI/MRSA resources Public health data and implementation resources HAI and MRSA risk can be translated into patient-facing prevention and escalation questions. Patients and families know warning signs and how to escalate concerns. Infection warning signs are missed, delayed, or normalized after surgery or discharge. Review discharge education, warning signs, call pathways, and escalation response. Use HAI risk alert reviews as a standing PFAC safety topic. HAI trends, readmissions, complaints, safety perception, discharge clarity.
