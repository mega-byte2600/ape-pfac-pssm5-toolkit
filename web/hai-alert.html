Risk alerts should be readable by the people most affected by them.

This page translates HAI and MRSA risk into PFAC questions leaders can use before, during, and after care. It connects Rosie Bartel’s story to practical infection-prevention, communication, escalation, and follow-up work.

Patient-first alert

Would my family know what to do if infection signs appeared after surgery?

For PFACs, HAI work is not only a technical infection-prevention topic. It is also communication, trust, discharge readiness, escalation, dignity, and whether the patient is believed when something feels wrong.

What warning signs are explained before discharge?
Who does the patient call if symptoms change?
How fast does the system respond?
What happens when the patient or family says something is wrong?

PFAC discussion guide

Questions for infection prevention and patient experience leaders.

Before surgery

Preparation

Do patients and families receive plain-language infection prevention expectations, warning signs, and escalation instructions?

During care

Listening

How does the care team respond when the patient or family notices a change, pain, wound concern, fever, confusion, or other warning sign?

Discharge

Transition risk

What does the patient actually understand before leaving the hospital, and how is understanding checked?

Follow-up

Escalation

How quickly can a patient reach the right person after discharge if infection symptoms appear?

Equity

Access barriers

Which patients are less likely to get clear instructions, timely follow-up, transportation, language access, or trust from the system?

Learning

Closed loop

When PFACs identify a risk, who owns the fix, what changes, and how is the result reported back?
