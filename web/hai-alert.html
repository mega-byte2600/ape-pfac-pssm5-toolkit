Healthcare-associated infections • MRSA • PFAC safety questions

Risk alerts should be readable by the people most affected by them.

This page translates HAI and MRSA risk into PFAC questions leaders can use before, during, and after care. It connects lived experience to practical infection-prevention, communication, escalation, and follow-up work without treating a patient story as outcomes evidence.

PFAC discussion guide

Questions for infection prevention and patient experience leaders.

Before surgery

Preparation

Do patients and families receive plain-language infection prevention expectations, warning signs, and escalation instructions?

During care

Listening

How does the care team respond when the patient or family notices a wound concern, fever, confusion, pain change, or another warning sign?

Discharge

Transition risk

What does the patient actually understand before leaving the hospital, and how is understanding checked?

Follow-up

Escalation

How quickly can a patient reach the right person after discharge if infection symptoms appear?

Equity

Access barriers

Which patients are less likely to get clear instructions, timely follow-up, transportation, language access, or trust from the system?

Learning

Closed loop

When PFACs identify a risk, who owns the fix, what changes, and how is the result reported back?

Evidence synthesis summary

Infection prevention is also a communication and escalation problem.

Healthcare-associated infection prevention is not only a technical safety program. For patients and families, it is also a communication, discharge readiness, trust, and escalation problem. PFACs can help leaders test whether infection warning signs are understandable, whether escalation pathways work after discharge, whether patient concerns are believed, and whether harm signals become system learning.
