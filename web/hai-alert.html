Current data signal

Use national HAI trends as the conversation starter, then ask for local data.

National trend

Several HAI measures improved from 2023 to 2024.

CDC reported decreases in MRSA bacteremia, CLABSI, CAUTI, C. difficile, VAE, and colon surgery SSI among acute care hospitals.

Exception

Not every risk moved in the right direction.

CDC reported an increase in abdominal hysterectomy SSI from 2023 to 2024, which reinforces why PFACs should ask about local patterns instead of assuming broad improvement.

Local use

PFACs should ask for the local dashboard.

Use CDC data to open the conversation, then review the local HAI trend, affected populations, post-discharge calls, complaints, readmissions, and patient education gaps.

PFAC discussion guide

Questions for infection prevention and patient experience leaders.

Before surgery

Preparation

Do patients and families receive plain-language infection prevention expectations, warning signs, and escalation instructions?

During care

Listening

How does the care team respond when the patient or family notices a change, pain, wound concern, fever, confusion, or other warning sign?

Discharge

Transition risk

What does the patient actually understand before leaving the hospital, and how is understanding checked?

Follow-up

Escalation

How quickly can a patient reach the right person after discharge if infection symptoms appear?

Equity

Access barriers

Which patients are less likely to get clear instructions, timely follow-up, transportation, language access, or trust from the system?

Learning

Closed loop

When PFACs identify a risk, who owns the fix, what changes, and how is the result reported back?

Standing PFAC agenda item

Translate infection prevention into patient-facing readiness.

PFACs can review whether safety information is understandable, whether warning signs are clear, whether escalation pathways work after discharge, and whether leaders report back what changed.
