Benchmarking • Design learning • Functional differentiation

Established PFAC resources provide strong guidance. This APE integrates them into a local leadership workflow.

This comparison examines authoritative federal, professional, and peer-reviewed resources. The APE does not replace those resources. Its distinctive value is the integration of evidence, CMS Patient Safety Structural Measure Domain 5 traceability, local population context, closed-loop action, measurement, and competency traceability in one applied product.

Established strengthsRecruitment, advisor roles, implementation guidance, representation, equity, and patient-safety engagement.
Documented evidence gapPFAC outcome measurement remains heterogeneous, and comprehensive patient-safety resources tailored specifically to PFACs remain limited.
APE contributionTranslate that evidence into a transparent current-state, action, ownership, measurement, and report-back workflow for a health-system setting.

Why this is more usable as an APE asset

Integration is the functional advantage.

Evidence

Evidence and limitations stay attached to action.

The evidence summary and matrix distinguish research evidence, implementation guidance, public Dartmouth Health evidence, local descriptive analysis, and lived experience. This makes it possible to see what supports a decision and what remains unvalidated.

Local context

Population data changes the engagement design.

The applied analysis uses municipality-level population, poverty, disability, and age data plus access and navigation signals to inform representation and implementation planning rather than leaving the toolkit generic.

Accountability

Patient input is connected to ownership and report-back.

The leadership tools capture what was raised, leadership disposition, accountable owner, due date, implementation status, evidence of change, and report-back.

Measurement

Contribution is measured before impact is claimed.

The toolkit separates operational and implementation measures from downstream outcomes, consistent with the literature showing heterogeneous measurement and limited causal evidence for PFAC-attributable outcomes.

Federal alignment

Domain 5 becomes traceable work.

CMS Patient Safety Structural Measure Domain 5 is translated into evidence-state review, accountable roles, gaps, and actions rather than presented only as a reference document.

Faculty review

Competency proof is visible.

The deliverables page explicitly connects selected Dartmouth and CEPH competencies to the artifact, calculation, or management tool that demonstrates each competency.

Claim boundary

Functionally stronger does not mean clinically proven superior.

The defensible claim is that this APE combines functions that are usually distributed across separate guidance documents, evidence reviews, local data files, and implementation tools. That is an applied usability and traceability advantage, not a clinical effectiveness claim. The site does not claim that PFAC activity by itself improves safety, experience, or clinical outcomes.

Can a reviewer trace a leadership recommendation back to its evidence type and limitation?
Can local population context change who should be represented and how participation barriers are addressed?
Can leaders document what patients and families raised, who owns the response, and whether the loop was closed?
Can the same artifact demonstrate public-health analysis, leadership, interprofessional integration, and engagement competencies?
