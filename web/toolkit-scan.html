Benchmarking • Design learning • Improvement gap

Existing PFAC resources are useful. This project adds a sharper leadership action loop.

The scan reviews federal, professional, state, and academic medical center resources. The purpose is to learn from strong examples while making the APE product easier to use for PSSM 5, patient-risk review, and closed-loop leadership action.

What existsRecruitment tools, meeting guidance, advisor roles, equity toolkits, and issue-specific PFAC exercises.
GapLess visible connection between PFAC voice, patient harm risk, leadership accountability, and measurable follow-through.
This toolkitConnects patient story, evidence, metrics, PSSM 5 structure, and action tracking.

Comparable models

What the project learns from and improves.

AHRQ

Patient and family engagement guide

Strong implementation guidance for hospital quality and safety. This project adds PSSM 5 framing, patient-risk questions, and a reviewer-facing evidence map.

IPFCC

Reflecting Our Community toolkit

Strong on representation, belonging, and equity. This project turns representation into a scored maturity question tied to patient experience improvement.

AMA STEPS Forward

PFAC implementation toolkit

Strong practice guidance for launching PFACs. This project adds Rosie Bartel’s lived-experience anchor and infection/escalation scenario logic.

Academic medical centers

Johns Hopkins and Massachusetts General

Useful examples of PFAC structure across service lines and hospitals. This project translates structure into evidence, metric, and accountability tools.

Patient safety

AHRQ MRSA prevention resources

Relevant to surgical site infection prevention and total joint replacement. This project makes infection risk a PFAC patient-first scenario.

Resource hubs

Betsy Lehman Center and Leapfrog

Useful for curated PFAC tools and issue-specific inquiry. This project makes the resource layer public, readable, and easier to extend.

Improvement thesis

The project is not just another PFAC starter kit.

The value is the connection: lived experience, infection risk, patient/family engagement, PSSM 5, care experience metrics, open resources, and leadership accountability in one practical toolkit.

Can the PFAC identify patient harm risk before leaders finalize a workflow?
Can leaders document what changed because patients spoke?
Can reviewers trace claims back to evidence and source type?
Can another health system reuse and improve the toolkit?
