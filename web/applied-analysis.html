Why this matters

Population context changes PFAC design.

A PFAC can be structurally present and still miss the people most affected by access barriers. The 2025 CHNA defines a 72,736-person primary service area and explicitly emphasizes populations facing barriers related to income, age, disability, and social or physical isolation. This analysis converts those local data into recruitment, participation, agenda, and accountability requirements.

Method

A reproducible descriptive re-analysis of public local data.

Source

2025 Community Health Needs Assessment

Town-level population, age, poverty, disability, and community access findings were extracted from the DHMC-APD fiscal year 2025 CHNA.

Calculation

Population-weighted exposure to above-average local conditions

For each of 19 municipalities, the analysis flags whether poverty, disability, or age 65+ exceeds the corresponding service-area average, then sums the populations of those municipalities and divides by the 72,736-person service-area population.

Boundary

Planning analysis, not a risk score

The derived percentages are descriptive screening signals for PFAC representation and access planning. They are not validated cut points, clinical risk estimates, causal findings, or measures of PFAC effectiveness.

The CHNA also shows substantial economic variation: median household income ranges from $58,750 in Dorchester to $163,654 in Hanover, approximately a 2.8-fold difference. The purpose of this comparison is not to label communities; it is to prevent a single engagement model from being treated as equally accessible across the service area.

Local access signals

The strongest operational signal is not abstract engagement. It is access and navigation.

The CHNA reports that 42% of community respondents had difficulty accessing primary care, while 54% of residents and 58% of community leaders said primary-care access had worsened. Mental health access was difficult for 35% of respondents; among those reporting difficulty, 68% cited long waits and 62% cited practices not accepting new patients.

72% of community leaders and service providers identified insufficient local capacity as an access barrier.
59% identified difficulty navigating the health care system.
55% identified isolated populations, including homebound or very rural residents, as not adequately served by local resources.

Applied proof

What changed in the APE.

CEPH 4: the project now includes a transparent, reproducible local data analysis with calculations and interpretation for public health practice.

CEPH 7: the target population is now explicitly defined using the 19-municipality Upper Valley service area, and local needs, assets, and participation barriers are translated into PFAC design requirements.

Implementation: the reusable toolkit is no longer only a set of blank templates; it is demonstrated against a current Dartmouth Health service-area problem using real public evidence.
