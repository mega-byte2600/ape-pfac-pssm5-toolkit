Executive summary

Evidence-Based PFAC Summary

My APE synthesizes PSSM 5 Patient and Family Engagement, PFAC evidence, and my APE Agreement into a practical leadership product.

My APE/ILE project translates the Practicum expectation for mutually beneficial applied work and a culminating high-quality written product into a methodology-agnostic playbook and toolkit for building PFACs across health systems. The two practical outputs are an environmental scan with annotated bibliography and a how-to guide/toolkit for launching and leading PFACs and PSSMs.

AHRQ’s PSSM Domain 5 centers patient and family engagement, including PFAC input on safety. PFAC evidence supports this direction, but also shows that councils must be operationalized through leadership deliberation, recruitment strategy, measurement, and documented action.

The project goal is therefore not simply having a PFAC. It is building a leadership-ready deployment model for co-production and co-design. Coproduction literature supports the idea that health care service is created through relationships and activities between professionals and the people they serve. Batalden and Foster’s Quality 3.0 framing reinforces the move from assurance and compliance toward service value and co-produced health.

My Chick-fil-A versus McDonald’s thesis makes the evidence practical. A health system can complete the transaction and still leave patients feeling rushed, confused, or unseen. The better standard is a learning health system where patient voice is continuously captured, interpreted, fed forward, and used to redesign care.

That makes PFACs a leadership structure for better experience, safer systems, equity, accountability, and organizational learning. This supports my selected APE competencies by translating evidence into practice, assessing patient and family needs, applying leadership principles, integrating multiple perspectives, and comparing engagement approaches by quality, safety, equity, and value.

Operating logic

What the summary means in practice.

Agreement

Evidence

PFACs need operating discipline.

The evidence supports PFACs when they are tied to representation, implementation, measurement, and follow-through.

Leadership

Patient voice becomes accountable action.

Leaders use PFAC input to redesign workflows, reduce avoidable harm, improve experience, and report back what changed.
