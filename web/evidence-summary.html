Executive summary

What the current evidence supports.

This APE translates the CMS Patient Safety Structural Measure, Domain 5: Patient and Family Engagement, current PFAC evidence, and the signed APE deliverables into a practical health-system leadership product.

The evidence supports PFACs as a structured mechanism for bringing patient and family perspectives into health-system decisions, but the evidence base remains limited for attributing downstream clinical, safety, or patient-experience outcomes directly to PFAC activity. A 2025 scoping study of 143 PFAC articles found wide variation in engagement and relatively few reports with measured project outcomes. A 2026 rapid scoping review focused on PFAC involvement in patient safety likewise identified a heterogeneous and still developing evidence base.

Recent evidence sharpens the operating model. A 2026 quantitative study of 203 respondents who worked with PFACs in U.S. hospitals found that leadership support was significantly associated with stronger measurement practices, more meeting content, greater decision-making involvement, and more input on policies and procedures. The practical implication is not that a PFAC alone improves outcomes. The stronger inference is that PFACs require executive sponsorship, defined governance, meaningful work, measurement, training, and closed-loop follow-through if leaders expect them to function as useful improvement infrastructure.

The toolkit therefore separates three questions that are often collapsed: whether a PFAC exists, whether it is meaningfully integrated into decision-making, and whether its work is measured well enough to learn from. That distinction is central to this APE and to the Dartmouth Health benchmark framework.

Leadership implications

What leaders should operationalize.

Governance

Give the council a defined decision pathway.

Document executive sponsorship, scope, escalation, committee interfaces, and where patient and family input enters policy, safety, and redesign decisions.

Representation

Design for who is missing.

Recruitment and engagement should explicitly examine under-represented populations, access barriers, compensation, language, disability, geography, and other participation constraints.

Measurement

Track contribution before claiming impact.

Use process and implementation measures such as agenda uptake, recommendations, decision involvement, action closure, policy input, and report-back before making outcome claims.

Learning

Close the loop visibly.

Record what patients and families raised, who owns the response, what changed, what did not change, why, and what will be reassessed.

APE alignment

Evidence is translated into applied decisions.

The APE uses the environmental scan, evidence matrix, Dartmouth Health benchmark framework, and leadership tools to interpret evidence, assess needs and assets, apply management principles, integrate perspectives, and compare approaches to engagement.
